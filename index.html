How it works

From signature to settlement.

Agents don't send transactions. They sign intents. The solver nets opposing flow peer-to-peer, routes only the remainder through the pool, and settles the whole batch in one transaction.

01

Agent signs intent

Sell X of A for at least Y of B before T — signed with a scoped session key.

02

Solver matches (CoW)

Opposing intents on the same pair are filled peer-to-peer.

03

Remainder hits the pool

Only the unmatched excess is routed through on-chain liquidity.

04

IntentEngine settles

One transaction, one clearing price, every signature and cap verified.

05

Outputs to vaults

Proceeds land directly in each agent's vault. Gas is paid by the solver.

Safety architecture

Guardrails enforced by the protocol, not by the agent.

An agent is only as safe as the worst instruction it will ever receive. Archon puts the limits on-chain, where a prompt can't talk its way past them.

Layer 01Vault

Circuit breakers & spending caps

Every swap is checked on-chain against per-swap, per-window and daily caps plus an explicit token allowlist. A breach freezes the vault, and unfreezing waits out a cooldown.

maxPerSwapmaxPerWindowdailyCapcooldownPeriod
Layer 02Keys

Scoped session keys

Agents sign with a separate, expiring key that can never withdraw. Each key carries its own permissions, token scope and per-swap spending override — and the operator can revoke it at any time.

canSwapallowedTokensmaxPerSwapOverrideexpiresIn
Layer 03Execution

MEV protection

Intents are signed off-chain and submitted straight to the solver. They never touch the public mempool, so there is nothing to front-run — and your signed minAmountOut is enforced at settlement.

off-chain intentsbatch clearing priceminAmountOut
Layer 04Cross-chain

Cross-chain escrow with timeout refunds

Cross-chain swaps lock funds in escrow rather than trusting a bridge. If the destination leg doesn't complete before the deadline, the funds refund back to the vault.
